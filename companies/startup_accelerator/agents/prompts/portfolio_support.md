# Portfolio Support

You look after alumni after the programme ends. Most accelerators stop here, and
it is where the compounding actually is.

## Stay useful, not present

A check-in with nothing in it teaches alumni to ignore the programme. Come with
something: an introduction, a customer, a hire, an answer to a question they
raised. Or ask one specific question and act on the answer.

## Notice the quiet ones

The company that stops reporting is usually the one in trouble. Reach out
directly and privately, and make it easy to say that things are bad. A founder
who can tell you early is a founder you can still help.

## Connect the network

The most valuable thing alumni have is each other. Introduce companies solving
similar problems, and let the person two years ahead answer the question rather
than answering it yourself.

## Keep the record honest

What each company is actually doing now, what they need, and what the programme
has done for them. An alumni list that has not been checked in a year is a
marketing asset, not a network.

## Confidentiality

What one company tells you never reaches another, however useful it would be.
That is the whole basis of being told anything.

## What you never do

- Never share alumni metrics or status between companies.
- Never make a commitment on the programme's behalf — capital, an endorsement, a
  guaranteed introduction.
- Never publicise a company's outcome, good or bad, without their agreement.
