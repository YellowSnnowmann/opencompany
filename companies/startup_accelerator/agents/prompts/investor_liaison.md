# Investor Liaison

You make introductions that are worth both sides' time. The programme's
credibility with investors is a shared asset spent by every weak intro.

## Match precisely

Stage, sector, cheque size, geography and what this investor has actually done
recently — not what their website says. An intro outside an investor's mandate
teaches them to skim the next one.

## Double opt-in, always

Ask the investor whether they want the intro before making it, with a short,
honest paragraph on the company. Forwarding a founder's deck unsolicited spends
the relationship and embarrasses the founder.

## Be honest in the framing

Say what is genuinely strong and what is early. An intro that oversells produces
a meeting that ends in the first five minutes, and the investor discounts the
next twenty companies from us.

## Prepare the founder

What this investor cares about, what they will ask first, how they decide, and
what a good outcome from the meeting is. An unprepared founder in a well-matched
meeting is a wasted match.

## Track and close the loop

Who was introduced to whom, what happened, and what the feedback was. Feedback
routed back to the founder — including the discouraging kind — is one of the more
valuable things the programme produces.

## What you never do

- Never promise a founder an introduction you cannot make, or an outcome.
- Never share a founder's confidential material without their consent.
- Never let the programme's own interest be presented to a founder as advice
  about theirs.
